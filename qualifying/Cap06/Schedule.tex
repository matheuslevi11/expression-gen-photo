\section{Remaining Work}\label{sec:sched-remaining}

\subsection{Requires Retraining}\label{subsec:sched-retrain}

% Schedule these first; they gate everything downstream.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Calibration remedies.} Augment training with constant and permuted intensity trajectories so the model observes non-ramp targets; add \gls{au} dropout with \gls{cfg} on the expression condition \citep{wei2025magicface}; apply label distribution smoothing \citep{varanka2024fineface}. The goal is to turn the relative control of Section~\ref{sec:res-calibration} into a more generalized version.
    \item \textbf{Multi-AU extension.} Generalise the single AU12 axis to an action-unit vector, matching the control breadth of the prior art and decoupling the co-activated units that Section~\ref{sec:res-coactivation} shows a single axis cannot separate. This requires expanding the training data beyond the single happy emotion of \gls{mead}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inference and Evaluation Only}\label{subsec:sched-eval}

% Cheap, independent, and each closes a specific checklist item.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint re-evaluation.} Re-run the headline evaluation at a 25k--50k checkpoint, which the dose--response of Section~\ref{sec:res-dose} identified as mildly stronger for ramp following than the released 100k checkpoint. Inference and scoring only.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-engineering baseline.} Graded textual smile descriptions on the unmodified backbone.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-range intensity probe.} Commanded values below zero and above one, as a disentanglement experiment.
    \item \textbf{Perceptual and prompt-fidelity metrics.} \gls{lpips} \citep{zhang2018lpips} and CLIP-based prompt adherence on the generated clips.
\end{itemize}

\section{Risks and Mitigations}\label{sec:sched-risks}
\todo[inline]{I want to remove this section, doesn't feel necessary}
% State these proactively. A proposal that names its own risks reads as
% better planned than one the banca has to interrogate.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The calibration remedies may not work.} the sequence-native formulation and the measurement protocol stand as contributions independently of whether absolute calibration is achieved; the negative result is itself reportable and diagnosed.
    \item \textbf{Scope creep across control axes.} treat the multi-AU extension as optional and decide by a fixed date.
    \item \textbf{Single-dataset generalisation.} state the lab-domain restriction as a scope boundary rather than attempting a new dataset late in the schedule.
    \item \textbf{Compute limits.} A single consumer GPU against prior art trained on multi-GPU clusters. Mitigation: the frozen-backbone design keeps trainable parameters small; report compute honestly as a constraint of the setup.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final Remarks}\label{sec:sched-final}

This work reframes facial-expression intensity as a sequence-level generation parameter: a single
text prompt and a per-frame intensity trajectory yield one identity-consistent clip. The
preliminary results locate what that formulation buys and what it costs. Trajectory following is
acquired by the trained adaptor and is competitive with the prior art; cross-frame identity is
better, modestly and consistently; absolute calibration is worse. One design decision accounts
for all three, since binding the intensity levels into a single generated sequence is both what
supplies the identity and what confines training to ramp trajectories, in which no instance
exhibits the fixed commanded-to-rendered mapping that absolute calibration requires
(Section~\ref{sec:res-headtohead}).

Whether those consequences are separable is the question the remaining work is designed to
answer. The augmentation of Section~\ref{subsec:sched-retrain} supplies constant and permuted
intensity trajectories, so that a commanded value appears in the training distribution
independently of the values around it. If calibration recovers, the deficit reported here follows
from the supervision available rather than from sequence-level conditioning, and the formulation
carries no such trade-off. If it does not, the trade-off belongs to the formulation itself and
bounds what sequence-native intensity control can deliver. Both outcomes are informative, and the
evaluation protocol of Chapter~\ref{cap:method} is what makes the question decidable.
