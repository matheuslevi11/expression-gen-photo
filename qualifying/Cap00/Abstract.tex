\noindent Diffusion models generate photorealistic faces from text, but controlling how much of an
expression is present remains coarse: text prompts are categorical, distinguishing ``smiling''
from ``not smiling'' rather than commanding a graded intensity. The systems closest to this
problem condition generation on continuous action-unit intensities, but they produce each
intensity level as an independent still, neither enforce nor measure identity across levels, and
report intensity control only qualitatively. This work reframes facial-expression intensity as a
sequence-level generation parameter: given a text prompt and a per-frame intensity trajectory, a
frozen Stable Diffusion~1.5 backbone extended with an AnimateDiff motion module generates a single
temporally coherent clip whose per-frame expression intensity follows the command. Only a small
expression encoder and the attention weights that merge its output into the backbone's temporal
attention are trained; the backbone is frozen. Preliminary results on a single smile axis, the
lip-corner-puller action unit AU12, show that the detected intensity follows the commanded
trajectory at a Pearson correlation of $r = 0.77$ over 120 clips, corroborated by an
independent detector that produced none of the training labels. Identity is better preserved
across a clip than across independently generated stills, at a worst-case adjacent-frame
similarity of $0.88$ against $0.76$. A head-to-head against the closest prior art ties on
trajectory following and is won by that system on absolute calibration: the control obtained
here is relative to the commanded trajectory rather than absolute, a measured consequence of
onset-to-apex training data. The contribution is therefore the sequence-native formulation of expression intensity, together with an evaluation protocol that separates trajectory following, absolute calibration, and cross-frame identity, and that guards the label--evaluation circularity.
