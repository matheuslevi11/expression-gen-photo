\section{Overview}\label{sec:method-overview}

\noindent\textbf{Task.} Let a \emph{clip} denote a sequence of $F=5$ frames that share one
identity and scene. Given a text prompt $p$ and a per-frame intensity trajectory
$(s_1,\dots,s_F)$ with each $s_t \in [0,1]$, the method generates a single clip in which
the detected intensity of a given action unit in frame $t$ tracks $s_t$; this work
instantiates the axis with AU12.
Because the proxy unit co-varies with its expression in the training data, the axis
controls a graded expression rather than an isolated muscle action. FineFace \citep{varanka2024fineface} instead produces the $F$ intensity levels as \emph{independent} images that share only a random seed; binding identity and scene across the trajectory by construction is the distinguishing property of this formulation.

The system reuses the camera-parameter conditioning architecture of Generative Photography
\citep{yuan2024genphoto}, substituting a scalar expression-intensity axis for
its camera axis. A frozen \gls{t2v} backbone, Stable Diffusion~1.5 (SD1.5)
\citep{rombach2022ldm} extended with an AnimateDiff motion module
\citep{guo2024animatediff}, supplies identity and scene coherence across frames through
temporal attention. A small trainable expression encoder converts the intensity
trajectory into a six-channel spatial conditioning tensor, which is merged into that
temporal attention through a set of zero-initialised weights.
Figure~\ref{fig:system} shows a pipeline overview.

\section{Backbone and Trainable Components}\label{sec:method-backbone}

The backbone is inherited whole and kept frozen: the SD1.5 UNet, the \gls{vae}, the CLIP text encoder
\citep{radford2021clip}, the AnimateDiff motion module \citep{guo2024animatediff}, and a
rank-2 RealEstate10K \gls{lora} \citep{hu2022lora} that is part of the released backbone.
Only two components are trained: the \emph{expression encoder} and the attention
merge weights that inject its output into the UNet. The implementation sets the
spatial attention processors trainable and then re-freezes every parameter related to LoRA, so those layers are a frozen backbone component and are not adapted. Table~\ref{tab:method-components} lists the components and the parameter
counts.

\section{Expression Conditioning}\label{sec:method-conditioning}

\subsection{Intensity Embedding}\label{subsec:method-embedding}

Generative Photography conditions the backbone on a six-channel spatial tensor of shape
$[F, 6, H, W]$ whose first three channels carry a \emph{physical} signal and whose last three carry a text-derived change signal (Section~\ref{subsec:method-ccl}).

For expression intensity there is no physical rendering to place in the first three
channels. The scalar intensity $s_t$ of each frame is instead broadcast spatially to a
constant $[F, 3, H, W]$ map. This scalar-broadcast design fits the inherited layout without
architectural change; spatially structured alternatives such as a landmark-displacement
map or a multi-dimensional \gls{au} vector are discussed as future work in
Chapter~\ref{cap:schedule}. Frames are rendered at $H\times W = 256\times384$.

\subsection{Change Encoding in the Text Branch}\label{subsec:method-ccl}

% TODO: Change the CCL term to expression instead of camera
The last three channels carry the \gls{ccl} signal, which encodes the direction and
magnitude of change \emph{between} consecutive frames. For each frame a prompt of the form
\texttt{<smile intensity: 0.25>} is formed and encoded with the frozen CLIP text encoder;
the per-frame CLIP hidden states are then differenced, frame $i$ minus frame $i-1$. The resulting difference tensor is padded to match the shape of the generated image, then broadcast across the three channels. The between-frames semantics are what make this signal specific to the sequence formulation: a difference of consecutive-frame text embeddings is meaningful only when the frames form one ordered trajectory, and has no counterpart in the single-image setting of the prior art.

\subsection{Injection into Temporal Attention}\label{subsec:method-merge}

The encoder output is merged into the backbone through a set of \emph{merge} weights added
to the temporal-attention path. This has a consequence worth stating explicitly: with no adaptor checkpoint loaded, the merge contributes exactly zero and the model is bit-identical to the unmodified backbone. That equivalence was verified on 2026-07-09, where running the frozen backbone through the
conditioning code path with zero-initialised merge weights produced output bit-identical
to bypassing the conditioning entirely. It is what makes the ablation baseline of
Section~\ref{sec:method-baselines} \emph{provably} fair: the baseline is not a separate
model but the same model with its trained contribution set to zero.

\section{Training Data}\label{sec:method-data}
% TODO: Review the underline text
Training data is derived from the front-view \emph{happy} clips of \gls{mead}
\citep{wang2020mead}. Faces are cropped with MediaPipe \citep{lugaresi2019mediapipe}, and
each frame is labelled with a continuous AU intensity from py-feat
\citep{cheong2023pyfeat}. Raw \gls{mead} provides three discrete intensity levels per
clip; \underline{the dataset composes onset-to-apex monotonic \emph{ramps} of $F$ frames from sorted frames}, so that a clip's frames increase in expression intensity. The split contains
4{,}028 training and 176 validation clips. The validation identities do not appear in training, to ensure there isn't an identity leakage into the validation signal.
% TODO: Review this section. I don't think the calibration results should be mentioned here
The training clips are ramps only, that is, onset-to-apex trajectories; the model therefore sees expression intensity only as a quantity that increases through a clip, and this is the root cause of the relative-not-absolute calibration result in Section~\ref{sec:res-calibration}. Second,
\gls{mead} happy clips carry substantial AU12 even at their lowest labelled level, which
biases the low end of the intensity range upward. Crucially, none of this data enters the
evaluation loop: all reported results are generated from a text-prompt bank and scored by
external detectors (Section~\ref{sec:method-evaluation}), so the training and validation
clips influence only the learned weights, never a reported metric.

\section{Training Setup}\label{sec:method-training}
% TODO: Extract the parameters to a table instead of parenthesis in the middle of the text
The training objective is the standard denoising-diffusion loss \citep{ho2020ddpm}: with
the backbone frozen, the expression encoder and merge weights are optimised to predict the
noise added to the encoded video latents, conditioned on the prompt and the six-channel
tensor. Optimisation uses AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $0.01$,
$\epsilon=10^{-8}$) at a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$ with no warmup and gradient
clipping at norm $1.0$. The effective batch size is $4$, realised as a micro-batch of $1$
with $4$ gradient-accumulation steps, under fp16 mixed precision; peak memory is about
$19$\,GB on the RTX~3090. Training runs for 100k steps, checkpointing every 1{,}000 steps
and sampling validation clips every 5{,}000 steps (capped at 8 clips), with a fixed seed
of $42$.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:method-evaluation}
% TODO: This section needs to be rewritten, it's not on scientific language
The evaluation is itself a contribution, because none of the four closest works
\citep{varanka2024fineface, wei2025magicface, hua2026pixelsmile, jiang2024emojidiff}
measures all three of the axes below, and all four score expression with the same detector
family that produced their training labels without flagging the resulting circularity. The
protocol separates three questions and answers each with its own instrument. Unless stated
otherwise, samples are generated from a bank of 40 text prompts (\texttt{eval\_prompts.txt})
with three seeds ($42,43,44$), at 25 denoising steps and classifier-free guidance
\citep{ho2022cfg} scale $8.0$.

\subsection{Trajectory Following}\label{subsec:eval-ramp}
% TODO: This section needs to be refined
The first question is whether the detected intensity follows the commanded trajectory. An
ascending ramp $(0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0)$ is commanded, the generated frames are scored
with py-feat, and the Pearson correlation $r$ between the commanded per-frame intensity and
the detected AU12 intensity is reported, aggregated over the prompt bank and seeds
($n=120$). A high $r$ says the model orders and spaces the frames as commanded; because
Pearson $r$ is invariant to scale and offset, it measures following, not calibration, which motivates the next axis.

\subsection{Absolute Calibration}\label{subsec:eval-calibration}

The second question separates ``follows a ramp'' from ``is calibrated''. The commanded
value is held \emph{constant} across a clip and varied between clips over
$\{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}$: a calibrated model changes its detected intensity with the
commanded constant, whereas a model that only responds to change does not. Following
\citet{hua2026pixelsmile} for comparability, the \gls{cls} is reported as the Pearson
correlation between the commanded constant and the pooled detected intensities, and it is
complemented by an absolute error (\gls{mse} of detected AU12 against the commanded value),
since Pearson $r$ alone cannot detect a constant offset. A permuted-list design, in which
non-monotonic trajectories are commanded, probes whether per-frame conditioning is
genuinely per-frame rather than a single ramp-shaped prior.

\subsection{Identity Consistency}\label{subsec:eval-identity}

The third question is whether identity is preserved across the intensity levels. Within a
generated clip, face embeddings are extracted per frame with an InceptionResnetV1 network
trained on VGGFace2 \citep{schroff2015facenet, cao2018vggface2}, and the cosine similarity
between frames is reported. The \emph{adjacent-frame minimum} is reported rather than only
the mean, because the worst transition is the one a viewer notices. The same measurement is
applied to the independent stills of the baseline, so the comparison is like-for-like.
This is a \emph{within-clip} coherence measure, for which high similarity is the goal; it
must not be confused with the source-to-output identity similarity reported by editing and
transfer methods, where an excessively high value instead signals that little editing
occurred. Multi-model averaging, adding an ArcFace embedding \citep{deng2019arcface}, is
planned and scheduled in Chapter~\ref{cap:schedule}.

\subsection{Guarding Against Label--Evaluation Circularity}\label{subsec:eval-circularity}

py-feat produces both the training labels and the primary AU12 metric, a circularity that
would let the model score well by reproducing its labeller's biases. The mitigation is a
second estimator from a different family: a label-independent smile proxy computed from
MediaPipe FaceMesh mouth-corner geometry \citep{lugaresi2019mediapipe}, which touched none
of the training labels. Where the two estimators agree, a result is not an artefact of the
py-feat pipeline. All four studied works share the label/evaluation circularity without
flagging it, so measuring against an independent detector is an evaluation contribution
rather than an admission of weakness; its limit is that the proxy measures smile geometry
only, not the full AU12 construct, and is reported as a corroborating rather than primary
signal.

\section{Baselines}\label{sec:method-baselines}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frozen backbone.} The same backbone with the same seed and no expression
    conditioning. It isolates the effect of the trained adaptor.
    % TODO: Think about better framing this
    \item \textbf{FineFace with matched prompts.} \citet{varanka2024fineface} run as
    independent stills over the same prompts and intensity levels. This is the comparison
    that isolates the sequence-native claim, since it differs from the proposed method
    precisely in whether the levels are bound into one clip.
\end{itemize}

Inference is seed-locked: the sampler is re-seeded immediately before generation, so the
same seed and configuration yield bit-identical outputs, and ablations that differ only in
construction-time random draws remain directly comparable. This is what allows the frozen
backbone above to be a strictly controlled counterfactual of the trained model rather than
an independently sampled one.
