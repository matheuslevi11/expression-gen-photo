\section{Overview}\label{sec:method-overview}

\noindent\textbf{Task.} Let a \emph{clip} denote the $F=5$ frames produced by a single
generation pass from one text prompt. Given a prompt $p$ and a commanded intensity trajectory
$s=(s_1,\dots,s_F)\in[0,1]^F$, the method generates a clip $x=(x_1,\dots,x_F)$ holding one
identity and one scene, in which the detected intensity $\hat{s}_t$ of a given action unit in
frame $x_t$ follows $s_t$ in the order-preserving sense of
Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}; this work instantiates the axis with AU12.
Because the proxy unit co-varies with its expression in the training data, the axis
controls a graded expression rather than an isolated muscle action. FineFace \citep{varanka2024fineface} instead produces the $F$ intensity levels as \emph{independent} images that share only a random seed; binding identity and scene across the trajectory by construction is the distinguishing property of this formulation.

The system reuses the camera-parameter conditioning architecture of Generative Photography
\citep{yuan2024genphoto}, substituting a scalar expression-intensity axis for
its camera axis. A frozen \gls{t2v} backbone, Stable Diffusion~1.5 (SD1.5)
\citep{rombach2022ldm} extended with an AnimateDiff motion module
\citep{guo2024animatediff}, supplies identity and scene coherence across frames through
temporal attention. A small trainable expression encoder converts the intensity
trajectory into a six-channel spatial conditioning tensor, which is merged into that
temporal attention through a set of zero-initialised weights.
Figure~\ref{fig:system} shows a pipeline overview.

\section{Backbone and Trainable Components}\label{sec:method-backbone}

The backbone is inherited whole and kept frozen: the SD1.5 UNet, the \gls{vae}, the CLIP text encoder
\citep{radford2021clip}, the AnimateDiff motion module \citep{guo2024animatediff}, and a RealEstate10K 
\gls{lora} \citep{hu2022lora} that is part of the released backbone.
Only two components are trained: the \emph{expression encoder} and the attention
merge weights that inject its output into the UNet. The implementation sets the
spatial attention processors trainable and then re-freezes every parameter related to LoRA, so those layers are a frozen backbone component and are not adapted. Table~\ref{tab:method-components} lists the components and the parameter

\section{Expression Conditioning}\label{sec:method-conditioning}

\subsection{Intensity Embedding}\label{subsec:method-embedding}

Generative Photography conditions the backbone on a six-channel spatial tensor of shape
$[F, 6, H, W]$ whose first three channels carry a \emph{physical} signal and whose last three carry a text-derived change signal.

For expression intensity there is no physical rendering to place in the first three
channels. The scalar intensity $s_t$ of each frame is instead broadcast spatially to a
constant $[F, 3, H, W]$ map. This scalar-broadcast design fits the inherited layout without
architectural change; spatially structured alternatives such as a landmark-displacement
map or a multi-dimensional \gls{au} vector are discussed as future work in
Chapter~\ref{cap:schedule}. Frames are rendered at $H\times W = 256\times384$.
\subsection{Inter-Frame Change Encoding}\label{subsec:method-ccl}

The last three channels carry an \emph{inter-frame change} signal that encodes the
direction and magnitude of change \emph{between} consecutive frames, adapting the
change-encoding construction of Generative Photography's \gls{ccl} \citep{yuan2024genphoto}
to expression-intensity prompts. For each frame a prompt of the form
\texttt{<smile intensity: 0.25>} is formed and encoded with the frozen CLIP text encoder;
the per-frame CLIP hidden states are then differenced, frame $i$ minus frame $i-1$. The resulting difference tensor is padded to match the shape of the generated image, then broadcast across the three channels. The between-frames semantics are what make this signal specific to the sequence formulation: a difference of consecutive-frame text embeddings is meaningful only when the frames form one ordered trajectory, and has no counterpart in other methods.

\subsection{Temporal Attention}\label{subsec:method-merge}

The conditioning tensor is injected into the temporal-attention layers of the motion
module. Let $h$ denote the hidden states entering one such layer and $f$ the corresponding expression feature produced by the
encoder. A trainable linear map $W$, one per layer, is applied to their sum and added back
as a residual,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merge}
    h' = h + \lambda\, W(h + f),
\end{equation}
%
with $\lambda = 1$ throughout. The merged state $h'$ supplies the query, key and value
projections of the layer, so the intensity signal conditions both what a frame attends to
and what it exposes to the other frames. This makes the per-frame intensity be read in the context of the whole trajectory.

Every $W$ is zero-initialised, so before any update Equation~\ref{eq:merge} reduces to the
identity map $h' = h$ and the conditioning path is inert. Optimisation thus starts from the
behaviour of the unmodified backbone, and the trained adaptor is a learned deviation from
a fixed reference point.

\section{Training Data}\label{sec:method-data}

Training data is derived from the front-view \emph{happy} clips of \gls{mead}
\citep{wang2020mead}. Faces are cropped with MediaPipe \citep{lugaresi2019mediapipe}, and
each frame is labelled with a continuous AU intensity from py-feat
\citep{cheong2023pyfeat}. Raw \gls{mead} provides three discrete intensity levels per
clip; the dataset composes monotonically increasing \emph{ramps} of $F$ frames by sorting frames by labelled intensity, spanning the onset-to-apex portion of each clip. The split contains
4{,}028 training and 176 validation clips. The validation identities do not appear in training to ensure there isn't an identity leakage into the validation signal. \gls{mead} happy clips carry substantial AU12 even at their lowest labelled level, which biases the low end of the intensity range upward.

\section{Training Setup}\label{sec:method-training}

The training objective is the standard denoising-diffusion loss \citep{ho2020ddpm}: with
the backbone frozen, the expression encoder and merge weights are optimised to predict the
noise added to the encoded video latents, conditioned on the prompt and the six-channel
tensor. Optimisation uses AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $0.01$,
$\epsilon=10^{-8}$) at a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$ with no warmup and gradient
clipping at norm $1.0$. The effective batch size is $4$, realised as a micro-batch of $1$
with $4$ gradient-accumulation steps, under fp16 mixed precision; peak memory is about
$19$\,GB on the RTX~3090. Training runs for 100k steps, checkpointing every 1{,}000 steps
and sampling validation clips every 5{,}000 steps, with a fixed seed
of $42$.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:method-evaluation}

Three properties of a generated clip are measured separately, each with its own instrument:
whether the detected intensity follows the commanded trajectory
, whether a commanded value maps to a fixed detected
intensity, and whether identity is preserved across
the clip . Section~\ref{subsec:eval-circularity} states
the control applied to the shared dependence of labels and metrics on a single estimator.
Unless stated otherwise, samples are generated from a bank of 40 text prompts with three seeds, 
at 25 denoising steps and \gls{cfg} \citep{ho2022cfg} scale $8.0$.

\subsection{Trajectory Following}\label{subsec:eval-ramp}

Trajectory following is measured under an ascending ramp with five steps from 0 to 1. The
generated frames are scored with py-feat and the Pearson correlation $r$ between the
commanded per-frame intensity and the detected AU12 intensity is reported, aggregated over
the prompt bank. Pearson $r$ is invariant to scale and offset, so it quantifies the ordering 
and relative spacing of the frames rather than the absolute intensity they attain.

\subsection{Absolute Calibration}\label{subsec:eval-calibration}

Calibration is measured with clips in which the commanded value is held \emph{constant}
across the $F$ frames and varied between clips over $\{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}$. Under this
design a calibrated model varies its detected intensity with the commanded constant,
whereas a model that responds only to change within a clip does not. Following
\citet{hua2026pixelsmile} for comparability, the \gls{cls} is reported as the Pearson
correlation between the commanded constant and the pooled detected intensities. Pearson $r$
is insensitive to a constant offset, so it is complemented by the \gls{mse} of the detected
AU12 against the commanded value.

\subsection{Identity Consistency}\label{subsec:eval-identity}

Identity consistency is measured within a generated clip. Face embeddings are extracted per
frame with an InceptionResnetV1 network trained on VGGFace2
\citep{schroff2015facenet, cao2018vggface2}, and the cosine similarity between frames is
reported. Both the mean and the \emph{adjacent-frame minimum} are reported, the latter
because a mean over frames can mask a single large discontinuity. The same measurement is
applied to the independent stills produced by the baselines of
Section~\ref{sec:method-baselines}, under the same prompts and seeds.

The quantity is a \emph{within-clip} coherence measure, for which a high value is the
objective. It is distinct from the source-to-output identity similarity reported by editing
and transfer methods, where a high value can instead indicate that little editing occurred.

\subsection{Label--Evaluation Circularity}\label{subsec:eval-circularity}

The AU estimator py-feat produces both the training labels and the primary AU12 metric. A model could
therefore obtain a favourable score by reproducing the biases of its own labeller rather
than by rendering the commanded expression. A second estimator from a different family is
used as a control: a smile proxy computed from MediaPipe FaceMesh mouth-corner geometry
\citep{lugaresi2019mediapipe}, which contributed no training label. Agreement between the
two estimators indicates that a result is not specific to the py-feat pipeline. The proxy
measures mouth-corner geometry rather than the full AU12 construct, and is therefore
reported as a corroborating signal.

\section{Baselines}\label{sec:method-baselines}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frozen backbone.} The same backbone with the same seed and no expression
    conditioning. It isolates the effect of the trained adaptor.
    % TODO: Think about better framing this
    \item \textbf{FineFace with matched prompts.} \citet{varanka2024fineface} run as
    independent stills over the same prompts and intensity levels. This is the comparison
    that isolates the sequence-native claim, since it differs from the proposed method
    precisely in whether the levels are bound into one clip.
\end{itemize}
